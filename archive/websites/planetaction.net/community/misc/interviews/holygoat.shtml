    Interviews: Holy Goat (A-Team)

    nova: First off, we'll start with the generic "Who are you?" question, ie; age, location, sexual preference, favorite food, family tree etc. :P

    HolyGoat: Well, I'm Rich, better known as Holy Goat, a mapper from the UK. I'm not too old, straight, like fried eggs, and have a perfectly normal family tree :)

    Mapping wise, you may have played Cinema or Asylum for AQ2, DayDam, ActCity, Asylum2, or DM_Dojo for AHL.
    You may also have had me consult on your levels, seen me on a server, read one of my tutorials, or seen my drivel in a recent Machinima article. Who knows, you probably don't!

    nova: You're the lead mapper for the A-Team, correct? What responsibilities does this position entail? Do you do anything else for the community besides map?

    HolyGoat: I'm one of the mappers for AHL (I suppose the lead, as I've been on the team for longest) and the lead mapper for AUT.

    For AHL, I've got a pretty wide brief... make levels as and when I like. For AUT, my only 'official' responsibility is to make lots of really good levels :)

    However, I also do the website stuff, write tutorials (at www.mapfinger.com and aut.action-web.net), make the AUT Texture Packs (aut.action-web.net), dispense mapping help, do custom textures, test people's maps, offer a helping hand to the AUT Amateur Mapping Guild, and do interviews :)
    I think that's quite enough!

    nova: What process do you go through to come up with your level ideas? Do you flesh out your ideas on paper, or do you just open WorldCraft and have a go at it?

    HolyGoat: Long periods with a sketchpad at 3am seems to be the main route I take to develop map ideas :)
    I come up with lots of ideas, with sources in film, the real world, books, etc. If anything in particular sticks in my head (gameplay features, architecture) I'll sketch it, and see what happens. If it starts to pull together, I'll lay down some brushes, and if that continues to go well then I start proper development.

    Alternatively, I'll just hit the editor with a vision and make it, straight out - that's how Dojo came about. The main room I made, complete and fully textured and lit, in a day of solid work, with no breaks to eat. The rest took a bit longer, but hey :)

    nova: Do any of the A-Team members have any say in what you map? How much do you value the community's input?

    HolyGoat: Usually everyone else keeps out of the way and lets me go :)
    Obviously, they get the first look for testing, opinions, etc., but most of the time nothing needs to be done.

    Community-wise, you can often find a thread of mine appearing on the messageboard with 'New map - comments and suggestions' in the topic :)
    It's good to get the actual players to voice their opinions on a level before you've done most of it!

    nova: Do you have any more AHL maps planned? Was Dojo your last AHL mapping effort?

    It really depends on the time I've got available. I'm focusing on AUT at the moment, but I've got a rework of Dojo in the pipeline (as well as an arena version). I may go back and fix up DayDam (and perhaps do the NightDam I originally planned), but mapping's time consuming and I'm a busy man!
    I think Dojo's a fitting way to put the dust covers on Worldcraft for the moment :)

    nova: What do you have planned for AUT? What new features will AUT have to benefit all mappers?

    HolyGoat: Ooh, lots of things! Personally, I've got a few maps on the go at the moment: one an example of Modernist architecture, which I hope will see the light of day; Jazz, my evening tower building map... with a twist of smooth jazz; and another which I have yet to unveil :)
    You'll see them all in due course.

    As you can see with the texture packs (there are three available already, and I'm putting the finishing touches on 4 and 5 now), we're aiming to make things easy for mappers. In fact, I went so far as to help set up the AUTAMG to help level designers.
    Feature-wise, I can't really spill the beans, but we're looking into improving the mover code to try and improve the door system, as well as ladders and the like.
    And RaVeN (our soundguy) has been doing some absolutely incredible work with Creative in 3D sound that's not been seen in the engine before... that'll be great.

    nova: What do you think of this whole SDK 2.0 problem? Do you think AHL will ever recover fully after being crippled like it has? Do you agree with people who think the A-Team should drop AHL completely and focus on AUT?

    HolyGoat: It's a bit of an obstacle, but one that's essentially unavoidable if Valve want to test out their new netcode. There are a lot of mod teams in a worse position than we are... at least we've got a 100% SDK2.0 version ready for release in the next few days, albeit without some of the stunts.
    If anything, this could be an opportunity - the community's been very supportive, and I've been seeing a lot of people voicing their opinions on the new patch; if we can get a version that's free of bugs and runs smoothly, then it could well be a resurgence.

    For those who think we should drop AHL completely... would it really be fair to abandon the community and give up? I don't think so.

    nova: Would you ever consider mapping for a gaming company for a living? What do you enjoy most about creating maps for people to play?

    HolyGoat: Of course, though commercial level design is very different to mapping at an amateur level; the deadlines do get to be quite a drag. And of course, you can't afford to waste 3 months on a hairbrained scheme that won't come to fruition!

    What I enjoy most... absorbing the player. There's something magical about joining a server and seeing people enjoying themselves without actually NOTICING your level... that means it's doing its job. They might say 'wow, what an incredible fountain!' - but they don't notice it's a level; they hardly notice it's a game.
    That, and the joint challenges of beautiful architecture, flow, optimisation, texturing, lighting, and sound. It's a wonderful thing to do :)

    nova: What was your involvement in AQ2? Do you still play AQ2 at all?

    HolyGoat: Well, I made the first user-made map for AQ2 (Cinema, before I joined the team), ran the AQMD Map Resources section for a time, played it a lot, was a member of one of the most successful clans ever (FEAR), and made Asylum for it. I cut my mapping teeth in QERadiant!
    I hardly have time to play AHL anymore, let alone AQ2; a pity, because it's still a great game.

    nova: What are your feelings on how the Action community became fractured after the release of AHL? Do you believe that AUT will bring the AQ2 and AHL camps together?

    HolyGoat: It didn't so much become fractured as melt in another community with all the blending that takes place; AHL attracted a lot of people that have never played AQ2.
    AQ2 is a very different game to AHL; the original designers of AQ2 agree that AHL captures the action-movie feel in a way that the original could not due to the limitations of the engine.

    AUT will be as much of an evolution as AHL was; some of the things we'll be doing will stun people who're used to the strafejumping Res Dog skinned BaseQ2 Male models in AQ2!

    We've had a lot of interest from AQ2ers and AHLers on the board, as well as people who've played neither - as with AHL, AUT will draw from all of the communities to create a unique group. But AHL and AQ2 will still exist; there's nothing to stop people playing all three!

    nova: Do you have any plans for future mod efforts after finishing with the Action series?

    HolyGoat: I'll be doing this until I find something better :) And judging by the way things are going, that will be quite some time indeed. There's life in the old goat yet!

    nova: Thank you for the interview. I look forward to your work in the future.

    HolyGoat: Thank you :) Keep an eye out for the next two AUT texture packs soon, as well as the release of AHL Beta3.5. Won't be long now!
